## AIBO Section Note

Date: 2026-08-05

Section 4 of the Berkeley-Heiserman paper is stronger now, but its role must remain clear.

The AIBO material is no longer the paper's center of gravity. Its job is subordinate and demonstrative:

- to show that the Berkeley-Heiserman vocabulary can still be used on a real preserved robotic corpus
- to prevent the paper from ending as pure historical reconstruction
- to demonstrate later analytic relevance without overclaiming direct historical lineage

## Current concern

The jump from `Move` to `Football` is probably still too large for a first-time reader.

`Move` shows monitored action and recovery clearly, but `Football` arrives with:

- a much richer mode structure
- more variables
- more transitions
- a more complex embodied control pattern

For a reader who is just learning how to see these scripts behaviorally, that leap may still feel abrupt.

## What to do next in the companion AIBO paper

- Add one or two intermediate examples between `Move` and `Football`.
- `Maze` remains the strongest current candidate because it introduces environment-dependent branching and local comparison without yet requiring the reader to absorb the full mode complexity of `Football`.
- Another useful bridge may be a script that shows repeated sensing and reorientation without the full multi-mode pursuit structure.

## Why this matters

The method depends on helping the reader learn a graded behavioral vocabulary. If the examples rise too quickly in complexity, the method looks asserted rather than taught. More intermediate cases would make the ladder from activation, to monitored action, to decision loops, to mode-based embodied control easier to follow.

## What this note is not claiming

- not that the AIBO section should become the paper's main novelty claim
- not that the Sony corpus proves a strict historical line from Berkeley or Heiserman
- not that the paper should drift back toward a Sony-centered contribution

## What this note is claiming

- if the AIBO section stays in the paper, it should teach the behavioral vocabulary more gradually
- its strongest contribution remains demonstration of later analytic usability
- the Berkeley-Heiserman reconstruction must stay primary
