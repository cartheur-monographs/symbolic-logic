# Third Journal Version

Date: August 5, 2026

## Current Assessment

The paper is now strong in originality, source recovery, and conceptual ambition.
It has moved well beyond a literature summary and now makes a distinctive claim:
Edmund C. Berkeley should be read not merely as a symbolic logician of machines,
but as a serious architect of embodied machine intelligence.

The paper is not yet at high-impact-journal level, but it is clearly moving in
that direction. At present it reads more like a strong, source-rich late draft
than a fully submission-ready top-tier journal article.

## What Is Strong Now

- The Berkeley case is much better grounded in primary sources.
- `Machine "Intelligence"` now supports embodiment, sensing, environment-facing
  intelligence, and not only learning.
- `The Construction of Living Robots` now does real argumentative work rather
  than serving as background color.
- `Squee` is now integrated as concrete hardware evidence rather than only as a
  colorful example.
- The Berkeley--Heiserman comparison is sharper and more defensible than in the
  earlier versions.

## Why It Is Not Yet Ready for a High-Impact Journal

- The paper still carries a very large claim-space at once:
  Berkeley interpretation, Heiserman comparison, cybernetic placement,
  critique of current LLM-centered robotics, and the AIBO demonstration case.
- The central intervention is strong, but the scope is still broader than ideal
  for a high-impact submission.
- Some sections remain dense and would benefit from one more compression pass.
- The paper still needs an even clearer journal-style statement of
  contribution: exactly what this article newly enables a field to see, test,
  or do.
- The contemporary robotics critique is interesting, but it still risks making
  the paper feel more stretched than focused.

## Recommendation for the Third Version

The third journal version should aim to become more concentrated, more explicit,
and more submission-shaped.

### Main goals

- Tighten the paper around its strongest center:
  Berkeley plus Heiserman as a design vocabulary for embodied machine
  intelligence.
- Reduce or discipline any material that feels like a second paper inside the
  first.
- Make the contribution statement more explicit in the introduction and
  conclusion.
- Improve pacing by trimming repeated formulations and compressing dense
  paragraphs.
- Preserve the strongest primary-source gains from the current version.

## Specific Revision Priorities

1. Narrow the scope slightly.
   The safest center of gravity is Berkeley, Heiserman, and the reconstructed
   design vocabulary.

2. Reframe the contemporary robotics discussion more tightly.
   It should function as a concluding test or lens, not as a competing paper
   inside the article.

3. Clarify the paper's exact contribution in journal language.
   The paper should say more directly whether it offers:
   historical reconstruction, conceptual reframing, a comparative design
   vocabulary, or a methodological proposal. It does all four now, but the
   hierarchy should be clearer.

4. Strengthen proportion and readability.
   Keep the claims precise and slightly more compressed, especially where the
   prose expands on a point the sources already establish.

5. Keep the AIBO material subordinate unless it becomes a separate article.
   It is useful as demonstration, but should not compete with the Berkeley--
   Heiserman core.

## Submission Strategy

After a third revision, the paper may be suitable for submission to a strong
specialist or interdisciplinary journal. It is not yet wise to assume a
top-tier, high-impact placement without that additional round of sharpening.

The best strategy is:

- complete one more serious revision cycle;
- tighten the scope and contribution statement;
- then target a strong journal with a good fit in history/philosophy of AI,
  cybernetics, robotics theory, or interdisciplinary history of computing.

## Bottom Line

This project now has real publishable promise.

The current version is strong.
The third version should be the one that turns that strength into a genuinely
submission-ready journal article.
