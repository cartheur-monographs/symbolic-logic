## Summary - Berkeley January 1952

This source appears to be Edmund C. Berkeley's article `Machine 'Intelligence'`, published in the January `1952` issue of *Astounding Science Fiction*. The venue is a pulp science-fiction magazine, but the article itself is not fiction. It belongs to the mid-century moment when magazines like *Astounding* mixed speculative fiction with serious popular discussion of computing, cybernetics, automation, and machine thought.

The article's basic move is to argue that intelligence is not mystical and need not be restricted to human beings. Berkeley treats logical operations, memory, control, sensing, and action as capacities that can be organized into machinery. In the context of this repository, that matters because Berkeley is not only discussing abstract symbolic logic. He is helping move symbolic structure toward physically organized systems that can sense, decide, and act.

The article is therefore important for at least two reasons:

- historically, it shows that early discussion of machine intelligence circulated outside academic journals and into popular technical culture
- conceptually, it helps bridge formal logic and embodied control, shifting attention from machines that merely calculate to machines that behave

## Title Note

The article is usually listed in bookseller and issue records as `Machine 'Intelligence'`, with quotation-mark styling around the second word, not as a plain title `Machine Intelligence`. That detail matters when searching for early uses of the phrase.

## Strict Hunt - earliest use of `machine intelligence`

The safest conclusion from a stricter search is negative and qualified:

- this Berkeley article is an early and important use of the phrase in a printed popular venue
- it is not currently safe to claim that it is the first recorded use of the phrase
- the earlier machine-intelligence discourse presently points to Alan Turing rather than Berkeley

What can be said with some confidence:

1. Berkeley's article is from January `1952`.

   Issue listings for the January `1952` *Astounding Science Fiction* identify Berkeley's contribution as `Machine 'Intelligence'`.

2. Turing was already publicly and privately working in what later commentators call `machine intelligence` before January `1952`.

   Jack Copeland's chapter in *The Essential Turing* says Turing was thinking about machine intelligence by `1941` and had circulated a wartime paper on the topic at Bletchley Park, though that paper is now lost.

3. Turing's surviving report `Intelligent Machinery` was written in `1948`.

   That is not the exact phrase `machine intelligence`, but it is unmistakably earlier evidence of the same discourse and of Turing's attempt to theorize intelligent machines before Berkeley's January `1952` article.

4. Turing's surviving postwar materials continue this line through `1950`, `1951`, and `1952`.

   His `1950` paper is titled `Computing Machinery and Intelligence`. Later editorial material by Copeland refers to Turing's `1951` lecture and radio broadcasts as work `on machine intelligence`, and the `1952` BBC discussion is described as the earliest known recorded discussion of artificial intelligence.

## Present evidential limit

The stricter hunt did not establish an earlier surviving printed source that can be directly verified, from currently accessible evidence, as using the exact standalone phrase `machine intelligence` before Berkeley's January `1952` title. What it did establish is this:

- Berkeley is not the beginning of the underlying discourse
- Turing is earlier in concept and in surviving documents
- the exact first printed occurrence of the phrase remains unproven here

One further refinement is now possible. The earliest publicly printed, verifiable use of the exact phrase that this search could confirm is still Berkeley's `Machine 'Intelligence'` in *Astounding Science Fiction*, `January 1952`. However, the currently accessible sources do not establish an exact publication day within January `1952`. In bibliographic practice this often appears effectively as `1952-01-00`, meaning the month is known but the day is unspecified.

So the strongest responsible wording for this repository is:

`Machine 'Intelligence'` in *Astounding Science Fiction* (`January 1952`) is an early popular printed use of the phrase, but not safely the first recorded use. Earlier machine-intelligence discourse is already visible in Alan Turing's work by the `1940s`, especially the `1948` report `Intelligent Machinery`, and possibly as early as a now-lost wartime paper from about `1941`.

If the question is narrowed to the earliest publicly printed, dateable appearance of the exact phrase that could be verified here, the answer is:

`January 1952`, exact day not established.
