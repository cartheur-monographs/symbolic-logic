# Paper Goal Summary

The paper's goal is to argue that Edmund C. Berkeley should be understood not just as a symbolic-logic or early-computing figure, but as a serious designer of embodied machine intelligence, and that this line of thought becomes even more interesting when placed next to David Heiserman.

More specifically, the paper is trying to do three things at once:

1. Reinterpret Berkeley.

It aims to show that Berkeley's real contribution is not only formal logic, but logic as a design language for machines that sense, remember, control, and act over time.

2. Clarify the Berkeley-Heiserman relation.

It argues that Berkeley provides an architecture of embodied control, while Heiserman provides a developmental route from reflex, to memory, to generalization. The paper is therefore not saying that they are the same thinker, but that they occupy adjacent positions in one broader design space.

3. Turn that historical reading into a live engineering hypothesis.

The paper does not want to stop at intellectual history. It wants to say that this architecture may still be usable now, especially for robotics and behavior-organized systems, and that the AIBO material serves as a bounded demonstration of that possibility.

In one sentence:

The paper aims to recover Berkeley and Heiserman as contributors to a still-testable architecture of embodied machine intelligence, rather than leaving them as isolated historical curiosities.

That is the current center of gravity of the draft.

## Berkeley literature ranking by relevance to the paper

The Berkeley materials currently rank, by relevance to the paper, as follows:

1. `Symbolic Logic and Intelligent Machines`
2. `Algebra in Electronic Design`
3. `The Construction of Living Robots`
4. `Light Sensitive ... Electronic Beast`
5. `Squee, the Robot Squirrel -- Construction Plans`
6. `Giant Brains or Machines That Think`
7. `Computers: Their Operation and Applications`
8. `The Programming Language LISP`

The practical takeaway for the current paper is:

- keep `Symbolic Logic and Intelligent Machines` as the anchor text
- use `Algebra in Electronic Design` to reinforce the logic-to-circuit claim
- use `The Construction of Living Robots` and `Light Sensitive ... Electronic Beast` to reinforce the embodied robot argument
